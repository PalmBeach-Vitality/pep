Peptide Storage Best Practices

7 min read · Storage & Handling

Proper storage is essential for maintaining the integrity and stability of research peptides. Exposure to heat, light, moisture, and repeated freeze-thaw cycles can lead to degradation. Following consistent storage protocols helps preserve purity and experimental reliability.

Lyophilized (Freeze-Dried) Peptides

Most research peptides are supplied in lyophilized form. In this state they are significantly more stable than when in solution.

  • Short-term storage (weeks to a few months): Store at 2–8°C (refrigerator) in a tightly sealed container.
  • Long-term storage (months to years): Store at –20°C or lower. –80°C is preferred for maximum stability when available.
  • Keep vials protected from light and moisture.
  • Allow the vial to reach room temperature before opening to minimize condensation.

Reconstituted Peptides (In Solution)

Once a peptide is reconstituted, its stability decreases. Best practices include:

  • Store reconstituted solutions at 2–8°C for short-term use (typically a few days to 1–2 weeks, depending on the specific peptide).
  • For longer storage, aliquot the solution and freeze at –20°C or –80°C.
  • Avoid repeated freeze-thaw cycles — these can accelerate degradation.
  • Use sterile, low-binding tubes when possible.

Light and Moisture Control

Many peptides are sensitive to light and humidity:

  • Store vials in the dark or in opaque containers.
  • Minimize exposure to ambient light during handling.
  • Keep containers tightly sealed to prevent moisture uptake, which can promote hydrolysis and aggregation.

General Handling Tips

  • Always use clean, dry tools when handling lyophilized material.
  • Avoid introducing moisture into the vial.
  • Label aliquots clearly with peptide name, concentration, solvent, and date.
  • Maintain a storage log for valuable or sensitive compounds.

Key Takeaways

  • Lyophilized peptides are far more stable than solutions.
  • Long-term storage is best at –20°C or colder.
  • Protect peptides from light, heat, and moisture.
  • Aliquot reconstituted solutions to avoid repeated freeze-thaw cycles.
