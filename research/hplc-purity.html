Understanding HPLC Purity

8 min read · Purity & Quality Control

High-Performance Liquid Chromatography (HPLC) is one of the most widely used analytical methods for assessing peptide purity. When a supplier lists a purity percentage (for example 98% or 99.9%), that number almost always comes from an HPLC analysis.

What HPLC Purity Actually Means

HPLC separates the components of a peptide sample based on their chemical properties. The main peak on the chromatogram represents the target peptide. The purity percentage is calculated by measuring the area of that main peak relative to the total area of all peaks detected.

In simple terms:

  • 99.9% purity means the target peptide makes up 99.9% of the detected material.
  • The remaining 0.1% consists of related impurities, deletion sequences, or residual compounds.

How the Percentage Is Calculated

Most peptide suppliers report purity using the formula:

Purity (%) = (Area of main peak ÷ Total area of all peaks) × 100

This is known as area-percent purity. It is the industry standard for research peptides, although it does not account for non-UV-absorbing impurities such as salts or residual water.

Common Purity Benchmarks in Research

  • ≥ 95% — Often acceptable for early screening or non-critical experiments
  • ≥ 98% — Standard for most research applications
  • ≥ 99% — Preferred for more sensitive or quantitative studies
  • ≥ 99.5–99.9% — High-purity grade used when maximum consistency is required

Why Purity Matters

Impurities can affect experimental reproducibility, binding assays, and overall data quality. Higher purity reduces the chance that side products will interfere with results, which is especially important in receptor studies, enzyme assays, and quantitative work.

Key Takeaways

  • HPLC purity reflects the relative amount of the target peptide versus other detectable compounds.
  • It is calculated using peak area percentages from the chromatogram.
  • Research applications commonly require 98% purity or higher.
  • Always request the Certificate of Analysis (COA) that includes the HPLC chromatogram.
