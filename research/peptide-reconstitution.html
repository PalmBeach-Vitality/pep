Peptide Reconstitution Guide

8 min read · Storage & Handling

Reconstitution is the process of dissolving a lyophilized (freeze-dried) peptide in a suitable solvent so it can be used in laboratory experiments. Proper technique helps maintain peptide integrity and improves consistency across experiments.

1. Choose the Right Solvent

The most common solvents used in research settings include:

  • Sterile Water (or bacteriostatic water) — suitable for many hydrophilic peptides
  • 0.1% Acetic Acid — often used for peptides that are less soluble in neutral water
  • Dilute Hydrochloric Acid — sometimes required for basic peptides
  • DMSO — used for highly hydrophobic peptides (usually followed by dilution into aqueous buffer)

Always check the peptide’s solubility characteristics before selecting a solvent. When in doubt, start with a small test amount.

2. Calculate the Desired Concentration

Decide the working concentration needed for your experiments first. Then calculate the volume of solvent required using this basic formula:

Volume (mL) = Peptide amount (mg) ÷ Desired concentration (mg/mL)

Example: To reconstitute 5 mg of peptide to a concentration of 1 mg/mL, you would add 5 mL of solvent.

3. Step-by-Step Reconstitution Process

  1. Allow the lyophilized peptide vial to reach room temperature before opening (reduces condensation).
  2. Gently open the vial and add the calculated volume of solvent down the side of the vial.
  3. Do not shake vigorously. Instead, gently swirl or roll the vial to dissolve the peptide.
  4. If the peptide does not fully dissolve, allow it to sit for several minutes or use very brief, gentle sonication if appropriate for that compound.
  5. Once dissolved, aliquot into smaller volumes if the solution will be stored.

4. Best Practices After Reconstitution

  • Store reconstituted peptides at 2–8°C for short-term use.
  • For longer storage, freeze aliquots at –20°C or –80°C.
  • Avoid repeated freeze-thaw cycles.
  • Label every aliquot with peptide name, concentration, solvent, and date.
  • Use low-binding tubes when possible to reduce peptide loss.

Key Takeaways

  • Match the solvent to the peptide’s solubility profile.
  • Calculate concentration carefully before adding solvent.
  • Handle gently — avoid vigorous shaking.
  • Aliquot and freeze solutions that will not be used quickly.
